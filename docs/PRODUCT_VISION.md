# The Sprocket experience

*A Fenton Lab product story — October 2026*

## A little companion with an actual personality

Sprocket began as an animated desktop fox. The idea wasn't to build a giant assistant that takes over your screen. It was to create someone small enough to feel at home there: a curious character who moves, reacts, works at her little laptop, and turns everyday computer interactions into something warmer.

Her appearance is part of her identity: dark charcoal and orange fur, expressive blue eyes, a mechanic's harness, and bright electronic accents. She is designed to be recognizable through different motions and future accessories. The character artwork is maintained separately from this public documentation repository pending the appropriate commercial-rights review.

## Three principles

### 1. Character without hidden control

Sprocket's gestures and animations communicate what the companion is doing. A typing pose represents an active request; a scanning pose should reflect a real authorized operation. An animation must never be substituted for evidence that a tool actually worked.

She is not intended to give herself permission to run commands, inspect arbitrary files, or bypass system protections.

### 2. Memory that respects ownership

Sprocket's working prototype keeps conversation history locally. Future release work includes clearer memory limits, customer backups, deletion/retention disclosures, and safe migration during updates. No promise of unlimited memory or third-party AI usage is implied.

We want the companion to develop over time without treating your history as disposable. That's why update recovery and data-preservation testing precede a public release.

### 3. Growth through Pocket Workshop

Pocket Workshop is Fenton Lab's planned official place for optional animations, devices, and capabilities. It is modular by design; third-party or unsigned executable expansion is not part of the present public promise.

A Workshop feature should arrive as a verified, versioned package with compatibility checks and a recovery path—**not as a hidden modification to the main application**.

Our first engineering proof is the **Mechanic's Diagnostic Device**. The proposed animation sequence lets Sprocket take out a compact electronic board, connect miniature probes, investigate an owner-selected file or folder, react to success or an error, then put the device away.

The motion is visual feedback for bounded inspection, not a hacking or exploitation capability.

**Engineering status:** the 0.7.4 U3 host updater has an isolated development test receipt. Independent Workshop package delivery of this device remains to be demonstrated and verified.

## What ownership should mean

The intention is a one-time purchase of the base companion, with exact product terms still to be decided. Fenton Lab plans free evolution improvements and character-motion updates for base owners. Optional added capabilities or content packages may be offered in the future, but neither their pricing nor their entitlement policy has been finalized.

There is no public sale, paid add-on catalog, payment integration, or automatic-update service live today.

## Where Lucy fits—and doesn't

Sprocket is an independent desktop companion created within the wider Fenton Lab work. Lucy AIOS and E.M.M.A. have different governance and execution responsibilities. Sprocket does not inherit their permissions or claim to be their replacement.

Possible future integration would need its own authorization, boundaries, and evidence.

## The standard for the first release

Sprocket should not go on sale until the complete customer experience has been tested: installation, first start, memory, consent dialogs, update interruption, rollback, uninstall, accessibility, commercial-art rights, dependency licensing, privacy disclosures, and independent release checks.

A polished demo is wonderful. A verifiable product is the goal.
