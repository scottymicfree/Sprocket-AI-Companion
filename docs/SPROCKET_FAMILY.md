# Sprocket Family: learning companion for ages 7–12

**Status (October 16, 2026): working development prototype, in owner testing.** Not released, not available for download, and not certified as child-safe.

## For parents
- **Protected dashboard:**
  - a parent PIN, with a short lockout after repeated wrong tries;
  - the dashboard locks itself when left idle;
  - every parent screen asks for the PIN, however it's reached.
- **Child profiles:**
  - nickname and age group (7–9 or 10–12) only;
  - choose which subjects each child can use;
  - set a daily time limit, how often breaks come, and how long they last.
- **Progress:** stars for answers right first time, completed lessons, and minutes used. Export or delete a child's data at any time.
- **Learning Library:** see [below](#learning-library).

## For children
- **Reading and science:** short passages with questions, hints and "try it away from the screen" activities.
- **Math practice:** problems suited to the child's age group, with hints.
- **Beginner coding:** steer Sprocket across a small board with FORWARD, LEFT and RIGHT. The board shows where a program went wrong.
- **Homework help:** step-by-step guidance and a worked example with different numbers. It never just hands over the answer.
- **Talk with Sprocket:** facts, jokes and questions from built-in content. It isn't an AI. It declines personal information and points children to a trusted adult when needed.
- **Sprocket as a learning companion:** she looks at the question, reacts to right and wrong answers, celebrates finished lessons and rests during breaks.

## Learning Library
1. **The package:** the parent opens **Fractions Adventure**. It is included with the prototype and needs no purchase.
2. **The plan:** the parent builds one, by default Math → Fractions → ages 7–9, two weeks, Monday to Friday, 10 sessions of about 15 minutes, starting with a short skill check. If a choice isn't supported, the builder explains why and suggests alternatives.
3. **The preview:** it shows the objectives, a dated schedule, sample questions, the progress rules, and "No AI service and no internet required".
4. **Approval:** the parent approves with the PIN. Only then does the plan appear for the child. Changing the plan or the package needs approval again.
5. **The sessions:** each one has a warm-up, teaching, practice, a challenge and a review.
   - New questions are made on the computer from ten reviewed question types: shaded shapes, choosing pictures, matching, counting equal parts, top and bottom numbers, number lines, comparing unit fractions, equivalent fractions, spotting unequal parts, and guided multi-step challenges.
   - Answers are checked with exact fractions.
6. **Progress:** each skill shows Not started, Practicing, Developing or Demonstrated.
   - Demonstrated needs several right-first-time answers across more than one session.
   - After a mistake the child gets a hint, then a worked example and a different picture. There are no penalties and no streaks to lose.
   - Sessions resume where they stopped, and a missed day never counts as a failure.
7. **Parent controls:** the parent can pause, resume, modify or unassign a plan, mark a session complete, or delete the plan.

## Boundaries in the prototype
- **Offline only:** no internet, cloud AI, analytics, advertising, purchases or camera.
- **Limits come first:** the parent's daily limit and breaks always take priority over study plans.
- **Data stays local:** nothing leaves the family's computer, and chat text is never stored.
- **Packages are data only:** only reviewed packages bundled with the app can be used; there are no downloads.

## Before any release
- Child-privacy legal review and parental consent design.
- Independent child-safety and adversarial testing.
- Accessibility testing.
- Educator review of the content.
- Packaging and support.
- Commercial clearance of the character artwork.
